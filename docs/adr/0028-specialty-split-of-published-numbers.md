# AD-28 — The published numbers were never oncology numbers

> Part of the TrialGuard architectural decision log ([index](../../README.md#architectural-decision-log)). Amendments are appended here rather than replacing what they correct.

## Decision

The oncology scope was not protecting a specialty advantage, and the headline numbers were never oncology numbers. 61 of TREC 2021's 75 scored topics never mention a cancer term and gold coverage is 1.000 in both groups, so the published recall@100 of 0.440 was always **81% non-oncology** by patient count and by gold volume, unlabelled. Partitioning the same patients and scoring each group with `end_to_end.score` unchanged -- no new data, groups composing to the published aggregate by construction, $0 because cache coverage was 1.000 -- splits the system in two directions at once. **Retrieval is 54% better outside oncology** (recall@100 0.4701 vs 0.3052), which is a corpus-density effect rather than a model one: 26k recruiting oncology trials compete for the same slots with heavily overlapping eligibility language while a neurology patient's gold sits in a thinner neighbourhood, and MedCPT is a general biomedical encoder with no compensating advantage in the crowded region. **The agent is better inside it**: the tiered-contract lift is 1.90x on oncology against 1.61x elsewhere, because oncology's pool arrives poorer (base rate 0.307 vs 0.420) and leaves more to filter, and faithfulness is 2.6x cleaner there (criterion unverifiable 0.0138 vs 0.0355, zero self-referential citations on 621 grounded criteria). End-to-end recall runs the other way, non-oncology 2.3x higher, because that term is dominated by retrieval. So the two halves have opposite specialty profiles and the aggregate hid both. The claim the thesis actually makes -- the tiered lift -- holds in **both** cells above the published 1.60x, so **nothing in the system's value depends on the patient having cancer**, which is a stronger generalisation claim than the repo was making. Stated with its limit: 14 oncology patients, so tier precision rests on 54 surfaced trials and the 1.90x carries roughly +/-0.4; the direction is supported by three metrics moving together, the magnitude is not tight. [`e3_findings.md`](../../data/reports/e3_findings.md)

## Alternatives considered

Fetching a second-condition corpus and hand-labelling it, which CLAUDE.md rules out and which this cohort made unnecessary once the topics were actually read; running the agent half at top-100 (7,500 assessments against a $2 daily cap, and pool depth is common to both groups so it cannot change the contrast); reporting only the retrieval split, which would have shown oncology as the weaker specialty and missed that the agent is the better half there
